The Dinner Table Syndrome

Apr 06, 2026 · implant audio daily life

There’s something that happens to me often, and I didn’t know how to name it until recently: the “dinner table syndrome”.

It’s not a medical term or anything formal. It’s just a way to describe a very specific situation: being at a table with several people… and not being able to follow the conversation.

Everyone is talking at the same time. Voices overlap. There’s background noise.

And at some point, you just lose it.

It’s not that you don’t want to pay attention. It’s not that you’re distracted.

It’s that you can’t process everything anymore.

You start picking up fragments: a sentence here, a laugh there, a random word that doesn’t fully make sense.

Sometimes you laugh too, not because you understood everything, but because you caught just enough.

Other times, you just stay quiet.

From the outside, it doesn’t look like much. It seems like you’re just sitting there, listening.

But inside, you’ve already disconnected a bit.

With a cochlear implant, this becomes more noticeable.

Listening to one person in silence is manageable. Even comfortable.

But when there are multiple voices at the same time, everything comes in mixed together.

There’s no clean separation. You can’t easily “focus” on a single voice.

And the effort of trying to follow everything is more exhausting than it seems.

It’s not immediate. It’s not like you suddenly hear nothing.

It’s more of a gradual drain. Little by little.

Until you reach a point where it’s not worth trying anymore.

And that’s when it happens: you’re still at the table, but you’re not really part of the conversation anymore.

I don’t see it as something dramatic. It’s just one of those things that changed and is now part of how I experience certain situations.

Sometimes I participate less. Sometimes I prefer talking to one person. Sometimes I just let the conversation pass.

It’s not always bad. But it is different.
