Before my accident, hearing well didn't mean anything special.

It was just something that happened on its own.

I didn't appreciate it. 
I didn't think about it. 
It was just there.

After the accident I lost my hearing. 
And that's when I started to understand **what it really means to hear**.

It's been nine years since I got my first implant: a Nucleus 6.

Nine years hearing the world through that device.

Nine years getting used to a different way of listening.

This Wednesday I'm switching it for a Nucleus 8.

And for days I've been feeling something I didn't expect: excitement.

It's not the kind of excitement I can easily explain to someone who always heard well.

For most people, a Wednesday is just a Wednesday.

For me, this Wednesday 
is different.

It's a new device. 
It's new technology. 
It's the possibility of hearing a little better than I've been hearing for almost a decade.

And that has me thinking about something I don't think about often: 
**everything I lost that night, and everything I gained afterward**.

I lost the way I used to hear before.

I lost taking for granted something as simple as a conversation, a song, background noise.

But I also gained something else: the ability to get excited about something as small as a Wednesday.

Before the accident, I never would have gotten excited about a device. 
About an upgrade. 
About hearing "a little better."

Now I do.

And it's not that I didn't appreciate hearing before. 
It's that **I didn't know there was something to appreciate**.

You don't value what you take for granted. 
I didn't value hearing well because I never had to. 
It just happened.

Now every improvement, no matter how small, feels huge.

I don't know exactly what's going to change on Wednesday. 
I don't know if I'll notice the difference right away, or if it'll be something that settles in slowly, the way it happened with the Nucleus 6.

But I know I'll be there, waiting to hear the world a little differently.

And I know that excitement, the kind you feel waiting for something as simple as hearing better, 
is **also something the accident left me with**.

I didn't ask for it.

I wouldn't have chosen it.

But there it is.
